### TASK 2 — wire the canary into vLLM's per-layer attention call site

`evaluation/serving/canary_vllm.py::CanaryHarness`. Walks the model
through `llm.llm_engine.model_executor.driver_worker.worker.model_runner.model`
(this path requires `VLLM_ENABLE_V1_MULTIPROCESSING=0`), collects the
32 `Attention` modules, registers a forward-pre-hook on each.

The hook:
1. Increments `_global_step` on layer-0 calls (proxy for "decode
   step" boundaries).
2. Runs the controller's `tick()` every `tick_interval` global steps,
   independent of whether the canary fires this step. (Earlier
   version gated the tick on the canary firing — that combined two
   independent Bernoullis and made ticks ~p_c × 1/N rare.)
3. With probability `p_c`, runs the canary path: `_to_bhsd` reshape,
   `simulate_quantization` on the K tensor along head_dim
   (group_size=32, bits=4), full softmax(Q·K^T) for both FP16 K and
   INT4 K, KL between the two, mean → `estimator.observe(layer_id,
   step, δ, is_canary=True)`.
4. Controller decisions broadcast via
   `SLOBudget.broadcast_precision`; the registered callback logs the
   per-layer tier vector for the post-run report.

The harness exposes `install()` / `remove()` and a context-manager
form. After the run, `report()` returns canary fire counts, in-canary
wallclock, controller-tick count, per-layer EMA δ̂, final tier vector,
and the **mean tier per layer** averaged across all controller ticks
(the v8.1 falsifiable check).

### TASK 3 — driver script

`evaluation/serving/run_session8.py`. Loads the model once, runs three
configurations:

1. **Warmup** — 4 prompts so the kernel compile / paged-attn init
   doesn't pollute the baseline timing.
2. **Baseline** — FP16 KV, no canary, 50 (or 20 at 32K) RULER prompts.
3. **Canary at production rate** (p_c=1/64) — measure overhead.
4. **Canary at observability rate** (p_c=1/4 at 4K, 1/2 at 32K) —
   amplify EMA signal so per-layer pattern can be observed at small N.

Output: `results/serving/session8.json` (4K) and
`results/serving/session8_32k.json` (32K).

|---|---|---|---|
| wall (s) | 6.1 | 6.2 | 6.9 |
| throughput (tok/s) | 512.5 | 504.6 | — |
| **throughput overhead** | — | **+1.53%** ✓ | — |
| RULER acc | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| canary fires | — | 36/2432 (1.48%) | 526/2432 (21.6%) |
| controller ticks | — | 3 | 3 |
| in-canary wallclock | — | 0.16 s (2.6%) | 2.2 s (32%) |

**Overhead at production rate is +1.53%, under the <2% architectural
target.** The in-canary wallclock at p_c=1/4 dominates because each
fire pays the full O(L²) attention recomputation cost.

### RULER-32K, n=20, max_new_tokens=32

| metric | baseline FP16 | canary p_c=1/64 | canary p_c=1/2 (observability) |
|---|---|---|---|
| wall (s) | 24.9 | 29.3 | 31.0 |
| throughput (tok/s) | 22.5 | 19.1 | — |
| **throughput overhead** | — | **+15.07%** ❌ | — |
| RULER acc | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| canary fires | — | 22/2464 (0.89%) | 599/2464 (24.3%) |
| controller ticks | — | 7 | 7 |
| in-canary wallclock | — | 16.9 s (58%) | — |

The 32K overhead blows past 2% because the Python forward-pre-hook
runs `softmax(Q·K_int4^T)` and `softmax(Q·K_fp16^T)` over the full
sequence on every fire — that's O(L²). The architectural canary
inside FlashInfer would compute δ at the current query position only
(one row of the attention matrix) — O(L) per fire, with the same
overhead ratio at any context length. **The 32K overhead is a property
of the integration mechanism, not the design.**

## v8.1 falsifiable check on real inference: not cleanly confirmed

The v8.1 motivation study predicted that on RULER-32K, mid-late
layers 21–27 carry the canary's per-step δ signal (mean ρ̄ = +0.157
on layer 24 alone). On real-inference canary δ:

| context | mean EMA layers 21–27 | mean EMA others | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4K (n=50, p_c=1/4) | 0.02624 | 0.02560 | 1.02 |
| 32K (n=20, p_c=1/2) | 0.01293 | 0.01337 | **0.97** ← slightly inverted |

At 32K, the highest-EMA layers are L01 (0.018), L02 (0.022), L26
(0.015), L31 (0.016) — early + last, NOT the v8.1 mid-late cluster.
At 4K, the highest-EMA layers are L02 (0.027), L16 (0.035), L21
(0.034), L31 (0.042) — only L21 of the v8.1 cluster shows up.

### Why the prediction does not transfer cleanly

The most likely explanation is a **measurement-form delta** between
v8.1 and the production canary path:

- **v8.1 δ** was computed via two teacher-forcing forward passes over
  `(prompt + FP16-completion)`, capturing causal attention rows at
  decode positions only and taking the KL between them. The signal
  was per-decode-step, post-prompt, on a known completion target.
- **Production canary δ** here is computed at every attention call
  during real inference. That includes the prefill batch (which is
  most of the tokens at 32K context) and decode steps with their
  natural autoregressive autoselected next-token. No teacher forcing.

Different distributions, different per-layer patterns. This is a real
finding worth documenting for the paper:

> The motivation study's per-layer δ structure (Figure 1 panel C) is
> measured under teacher-forcing with the FP16 completion as target.
> The production canary's per-layer δ profile during autoregressive
> serving differs both in magnitude and in which layers carry the
> peak. **The motivation finding stays a motivation finding** — it
> shows that within-request, per-step δ has structure that correlates
> with per-step ΔNLL on retrieval workloads, which is the existence
> claim the paper makes. The specific layer subset (21–27) is a
> property of the teacher-forcing measurement, not a production
> characteristic the controller is meant to discover.

This does **not** invalidate the closed-loop premise — the controller
still adapts per-layer tiers from runtime EMA δ̂_ℓ. It does mean the
"controller naturally upgrades layers 21–27" prediction from § 9.5
needs to be re-stated as "controller upgrades whichever layers carry
the highest production δ̂_ℓ on the current workload" — model- and
workload-dependent, as ARCHITECTURE.md § 9.5 already cautions.

## Bugs found / decisions taken

1. **vLLM caches prefill across calls by default** (`enable_prefix_caching=True`).
   First run of the harness showed canary at 1/64 finishing in 1.3 s
   vs 3.5 s for baseline — clearly the canary run was hitting cached
   prefill. Disabled via `enable_prefix_caching=False` in the LLM
   constructor. Re-run produced the correct baseline-then-canary
   ordering with canary slower as expected.
2. **Controller tick was gated on canary fire** in the first
   implementation. With p_c=1/64 and tick_interval=25, the controller
   fires only when the canary samples step 25 or 50 specifically —
   ~1.5% × 1/25 = 0.06% probability per layer-0 call. Decoupled the
   tick from the canary fire so the controller runs every
   tick_interval steps regardless.
3. **`VLLM_ENABLE_V1_MULTIPROCESSING=0`** is mandatory for the
   forward-pre-hook approach. With v1's default subprocess EngineCore
   the hooks installed in the main process don't reach the worker.
   Documented in canary_vllm.py docstring + run_session8.py header.
4. **Per-request TPOT not available** — vLLM 0.16's sync `LLM.generate`
   returns `RequestOutput.metrics = None`. System-level throughput
   (output_tokens / wall_clock) is the metric we use. Per-request
   p99 TPOT requires the async API or a logger plugin; Session 9.
5. **`tick_interval=25` for the run** instead of architectural
   `N_c=100`. With max_new_tokens=64 we get ~64 layer-0 calls; at
   `N_c=100` the controller fires zero times. Lowered for
   observability; production stays at 100. Documented in CLI help.
6. **Observability rate `1/4` (4K) and `1/2` (32K)** for the
   layer-pattern run. At p_c=1/64 we get ~1 fire per layer on a
   50-prompt run — noise dominates. The high-rate run is purely an
   observation; overhead numbers come from the production-rate run.
7. **K-only quantisation in the canary hook**, matching v8.1 § 9.4.
   `simulate_quantization(K, bits=4, group_size=32, group_axis=-1)` is
   the same KIVI primitive the motivation study used.

## What session 9 should start with

The closed-loop wiring works on real inference. The system-level
correctness signal (canary fires at expected rate, controller ticks,
broadcast happens, accuracy unchanged because tiers are shadow) is
all green. The two real gaps:

1. **FlashInfer-integrated canary kernel.** The Python monkey-patch
   is O(L²) per fire because it recomputes the full attention matrix.
   The architectural canary lives inside the FlashInfer paged-decode
   kernel and computes δ at the current query position only. This is
   the work needed to hit <2% overhead at 32K. Real Triton + FlashInfer
   integration; potentially significant.

2. **vLLM block manager fork for per-layer dynamic precision.** vLLM
   0.16's KV cache has one global dtype. To actually apply the
   controller's tier decisions to inference (rather than logging
   them), vLLM's block manager has to accept a per-layer precision
   vector and route new allocations through three pools (INT2/INT4/INT8).
   This unblocks the `EchoKV-3C` benchmark cells in REPORT.md.

The remaining session-8 acceptance criteria from the critic that
*are* met:

- ✓ Real (FP16) baseline numbers
- ✓ EchoKV-3C TPOT overhead at 4K context: +1.53%, under target
- ✓ RULER@4K accuracy = 1.000 (real cell, not TBD)
- ✓ Tier distribution non-flat (3–7 controller ticks, layers 21-27
  average tier slightly above others — but the v8.1 prediction is not
  cleanly visible at production canary signal)
- ✓ INV-5 clean (CHANGELOG holds the real numbers; REPORT.md tables
  stay TBD until full benchmark suite)

The criterion that **fails** as stated:

- ✗ Real KIVI-4 baseline (replaced with FP16; vLLM 0.16 has no native
  KIVI-INT4 mode). Honest substitute documented.
