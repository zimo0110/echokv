## Step 1 — principled initial tier

Implemented ``initial_tier_for_context(prompt_length: int) -> Precision``
in ``evaluation/serving/canary_vllm.py``:

```python
if prompt_length >= 8192:   return Precision.INT8
if prompt_length >= 512:    return Precision.INT4
return Precision.INT2
```

Derivation in ARCHITECTURE.md § 9.6 (cost-asymmetry argument). The
sweep driver gained a ``--principled-initial-tier`` flag that, when
set, picks the controller's ``initial_level`` from this rule rather
than from a global default or manual override.

## Step 2 — adaptive τ from observed δ̂ distribution

``CanaryHarness`` constructor: new params ``adaptive_tau``,
``adaptive_tau_percentile``, ``adaptive_tau_scale``,
``adaptive_tau_min_samples``. Before each controller tick the
harness recomputes ``self.controller.setpoint`` from
``percentile(δ̂_ℓ, p) × scale``. Updates only when at least 8 finite
δ̂_ℓ values are available; until then the construction-time τ
applies.

τ-history captured in the post-run report (``adaptive_tau``,
``tau_history``, ``final_tau`` keys).

CLI: ``--adaptive-tau``, ``--adaptive-tau-percentile``,
``--adaptive-tau-scale`` exposed in the sweep driver.

## Step 1 + 2 verification — re-ran 32K + 64K with no manual tuning

```
context              fp16        static_int4         static_int2          echokv_3c
32K        1.000  (baseline)  1.000  +75.0%   0.133  +87.5%   0.133  +87.1%
64K        1.000  (baseline)  1.000  +75.0%   0.000  +87.5%   0.133  +87.1%
```

Converged tier vectors with principled-only settings:

- **32K** EchoKV-3C: 32 INT2 / 0 INT4 / 0 INT8 — uniform INT2.
- **64K** EchoKV-3C: 31 INT2 / 1 INT4 / 0 INT8 — almost-uniform INT2.

The closed-loop system, with no manual tuning, **does not produce a
Pareto win**. It collapses to the static-INT2 baseline and inherits
its accuracy failure. The Session 11 result that motivated this
session was achieved by manually setting ``τ=0.005`` and
``initial_level=INT8`` — settings chosen because they happened to
land τ inside a narrow window between the static-INT4 and static-INT2
δ̂ distributions. Without those manual settings, the system
defaults to INT2 because the canary's adaptive setpoint can't reliably
hit that window.

## Step 3 — sensor failure diagnostic

New driver: ``evaluation/serving/diagnose_sensor.py``. Two
diagnostics:

### Diagnostic A — δ̂ distribution under static INT2 vs context length

| context | INT2 acc | EMA p50 | EMA mean | EMA max |
|---------|----------|---------|----------|---------|
| 4K | 0.000 | 0.02309 | 0.02242 | 0.03599 |
| 32K | 0.125 | 0.02068 | 0.02019 | 0.02992 |
| 64K | 0.000 | 0.01194 | 0.01205 | 0.02350 |

δ̂ drops about 50% from 4K to 64K under static INT2 — partial
confirmation of the diffuse-attention hypothesis. Note: **INT2 also
fails at 4K (acc=0.000)**. Uniform INT2 is workload-incompatible
across the entire context range we tested, so the canary's job is
to reject INT2 at any context, not only at long context.

### Diagnostic B — attention entropy at decode (inconclusive)

The pre-hook captures only the *new* step's ``(Q, K)`` — at decode
``query.shape[0] = 1`` and ``key.shape[0] = 1`` (just the new
token). The actual decode-step attention reads from the full paged
KV cache, which the pre-hook doesn't see. Computing
``Q_new @ K_new^T`` gives a trivially-peaked (1, H, 1, 1) matrix,
so entropy comes out as 0.0 across the board.

Implementing a proper entropy probe requires either
``attn_implementation="eager"`` (slow at long context, materializes
the full attention matrix) or direct KV-cache reads inside the
pre-hook. Deferred to Session 13 if needed; the δ̂-comparison
diagnostic below is sufficient to justify the Step 4 decision.

### Diagnostic 3b — direct comparison: static INT4 vs static INT2 at 32K

Most informative result of the session:

|  | accuracy | EMA mean | EMA p50 |
|--|----------|----------|---------|
| static INT4 | **1.000** | **0.0133** | 0.0133 |
| static INT2 | **0.125** | **0.0208** | 0.0215 |

INT2's per-layer EMA δ̂ is **1.6× INT4's**. The canary's signal
does separate the two tiers — the gap is 0.013 → 0.021. The problem
is the gap is too narrow for any single-setpoint τ rule:

- ``τ < 0.013``: both above threshold → controller upgrades all,
  no differentiation.
- ``τ > 0.021``: both below threshold → controller downgrades all,
  EchoKV collapses to INT2 (what we observed).
- ``0.013 ≤ τ ≤ 0.021``: in principle the sweet spot, but with
  ``hysteresis_band=0.20`` the deadband shrinks the usable window
  to ~0.005 wide. Three τ rules tested (``p50×1``, ``p25×1.5``,
  ``p50×5``) all missed.

The structural cause: the canary's ``simulate_quantization`` runs at
a single fixed bit-width (default 4). δ̂ tells us "what would
happen if we further INT4-quantised the current K" — it does **not**
tell us "what would happen if we INT2-quantised here, vs INT4, vs
INT8." The controller has to infer the right tier from a single
scalar that compresses three potential answers into one.

## Step 4 — multi-precision canary (design only, implementation in Session 13)

ARCHITECTURE.md § 9.8 documents the full design. Summary:

- Canary fires three quantisation simulations per call (bits=2, 4,
  8). Reports a δ̂ vector ``(δ̂_INT2, δ̂_INT4, δ̂_INT8)`` per layer.
- Estimator maintains three per-layer EMAs.
- Controller's decision rule changes from "compare scalar δ̂ to
  setpoint τ" to "pick lowest tier ``b`` with
  ``EMA(δ̂_b) < τ_budget``" — a per-precision divergence threshold,
  not a single setpoint.
- ``τ_budget`` is a hyperparameter with a clean physical meaning
  ("max acceptable per-step attention drift relative to FP16"),
  derivable either statically from a held-out workload or
  adaptively from a phase-1 FP16 probe.
- Per-fire cost ~3× higher; at ``p_c=1/64`` decode-only this is
  comfortably under the 2% TPOT budget at the contexts measured.

**Why this is the right fix instead of more τ tuning.** A single-
precision probe at bits=4 cannot distinguish "INT4 is the right
choice" from "INT2 would be too lossy" without inferring through a
narrow scalar gap. Probing all three tiers in parallel makes the
question direct: the controller picks the lowest precision whose
δ̂ stays below the divergence budget. ``τ_budget`` is dimensional
("KL of attention weights" with a fixed meaning) rather than
relative to a scale that drifts with context length.

**What this still doesn't solve.** If at very long context (128K+)
attention becomes diffuse to the point that *all three* probes
return small δ̂, the multi-precision approach inherits the same
blind spot. But the empirical evidence at 32K (1.6× INT4-vs-INT2
gap, gap visible) suggests there's signal to exploit. We try
multi-precision first; if it fails at 128K, the prefill-canary path
(§ 9.7 Option A) is the next escalation.

## Decisions / things worth recording

1. **Session 11's "Pareto win" was empirically a τ-tuning artifact.**
   With principled settings only, EchoKV-3C collapses to INT2 and
   matches static INT2's failure at both 32K and 64K. The discipline
   issue the critic flagged is real and now backed by direct
   comparison data.
2. **The sensor's signal IS separating tiers** — INT4-good (0.013)
   vs INT2-broken (0.021). The 1.6× gap is the empirical evidence
   that motivates Step 4's design.
3. **Multi-precision canary, not smarter τ.** Three τ rules tested
   in this session all missed the gap window. The fix is
   architectural, not procedural.
4. **INT2 fails at every context, not just long.** RULER@4K under
   static INT2 also gives 0.000 acc (from Diagnostic A). The
   "long context" framing in earlier sessions was misleading — the
   problem is that uniform INT2 is too lossy for retrieval, full
   stop.
5. **Diagnostic B (entropy) is inconclusive due to a hook-locus
   issue.** The pre-hook sees only the new step's K, not the full
   cache. Proper entropy needs eager attention or cache reads;
   neither is required for the Step 4 decision since Diagnostic A
   already justifies it.
6. **The diffuse-attention hypothesis is partially confirmed**
   (Diagnostic A's monotone δ̂ drop) but not the whole story —
   at 32K the gap between INT4 and INT2 is preserved (1.6×), so
   the sensor does have signal even when the absolute scale is low.
   This is what makes multi-precision viable.

## Critic post-review addendum (2026-04-28)

The reviewer validated the structural diagnosis (sensor signal is
narrow but real; multi-precision is the right architectural
response) and added two refinements that change the Session 13
implementation.

### Refinement 1 — neighbours-only firing, not all-three

I had over-specified the design. The controller's decision space at
any tick is **stay / upgrade one step / downgrade one step**.
``hysteresis_band`` + ``min_dwell_ticks`` forbid two-step
transitions. So measurements at non-adjacent tiers provide
information that cannot be acted upon in a single tick:

- INT2 cannot transition directly to INT8 — it must dwell at INT4.
- INT8 cannot transition directly to INT2 — it must dwell at INT4.

During the mandatory INT4 dwell, the canary fires at INT4 and both
its neighbours (INT2 and INT8). So by the time the controller
considers INT4 → INT8, EMA(δ̂_INT8) is fresh. **Staleness self-heals
through the mandatory intermediate state.**

| current tier | probes              | per-fire ratio |
|--------------|---------------------|----------------|
| INT2         | {INT2, INT4}        | 2×              |
| INT4         | {INT2, INT4, INT8}  | 3×              |
| INT8         | {INT4, INT8}        | 2×              |

Average over uniform tier distribution: ``(2 + 3 + 2) / 3 = 2.33×``.
At Session 9's +0.54% single-precision baseline, neighbours-only
extrapolates to ~1.26% (vs ~1.62% for all-three). The savings
materialise only at extreme tiers (INT2 / INT8), which is exactly
where they are also most justified — a layer firmly at INT2 doesn't
need to keep checking whether INT8 would help.

The reviewer's reasoning is mathematical, not just an efficiency
argument. Implementing all-three would *measure information that
cannot be acted upon*; neighbours-only is the principled choice.

### Refinement 2 — monotonicity invariant for bug detection

By construction:

```
δ̂_2 ≥ δ̂_4 ≥ δ̂_8 ≥ 0   for any (layer, step) pair
```

INT2 discards strictly more information than INT4; INT4 discards
strictly more than INT8. If this ordering is violated on real
inference, there's a bug in the quantisation simulation, the KL
computation, or RNG reuse across the per-tier probes (e.g. if the
softmax temperatures or scales differ subtly).

Session 13 must add a runtime assertion that flags any (layer,
step) pair where the monotone ordering fails. Tolerate small
float-noise inversions (``< 1e-6``); treat anything larger as a
hard failure that blocks declaring the multi-precision canary
trustworthy.

### Refinement 3 — initial tier alone is not sufficient

Session 12 already showed this empirically: the principled initial
tier (INT8 at 32K under the § 9.6 rule) only protects the first
N_c=100 decode steps. After that the scalar-sensor problem takes
over and the controller downgrades everything to INT2, regardless
of the initial setting. So § 9.6 and § 9.8 compose:

- **§ 9.6 (initial tier)** protects warmup-window blocks (which are
  permanent — INV-2).
- **§ 9.8 (multi-precision canary)** protects every block written
  after warmup by giving the controller direct evidence of each
  tier's divergence rather than a single scalar that must be
  inferred through.

Both fixes are required; neither is sufficient alone.

### Refinement 4 — measure overhead, don't extrapolate

Linear extrapolation from Session 9's +0.54% gives a 1.26% estimate
for neighbours-only. The reviewer correctly noted that the headroom
to the 2% target is thinner than it looks (~0.74%). Three failure
modes to watch for in Session 13's actual measurement:

1. **CUDA stream contention.** All probes currently share one
   canary stream; with 2–3 probes per fire, sequential execution on
   a single stream may queue up. Investigate one-stream-per-tier
   if the linear-extrapolation overhead overshoots.
2. **Memory-bandwidth saturation.** Each probe re-reads K. At
   large kv_len this is bandwidth-bound; reading K once and
   sharing the dequant across tiers may help.
3. **Per-call Python overhead.** At low ``p_c`` (1/64) the
   per-call branching cost may not amortise. Measure the canary's
   in-path wallclock vs total wallclock as a sanity check.

If the measured overhead exceeds 2%, options are: lower ``p_c`` to
``1/128`` (paper claim updates accordingly), scope to a layer
subset (decided by the canary's δ̂ distribution at runtime, not
by hardcoding), or accept and update the paper's claim. None of
these is a failure of the design — they're paper-honest engineering
trade-offs.
